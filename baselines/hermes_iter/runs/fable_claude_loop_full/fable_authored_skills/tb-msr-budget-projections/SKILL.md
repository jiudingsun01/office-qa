# Treasury Bulletin: budget projections (MSR/Budget estimates) vs. actuals

## Where the data lives
- Each issue's **"Federal Fiscal Operations"** section (very front of the Bulletin) opens with a narrative + summary table of total receipts, outlays, and the surplus/deficit. It shows **actuals for completed fiscal years** and **estimates for the current/budget fiscal years**.
- Estimate vintages rotate with the federal budget cycle:
  - **March issue** → estimates from the President's **Budget** (released ~February).
  - **September issue** → estimates from the **Mid-Session Review (MSR)** (released ~July/August).
- So for a fiscal year Y:
  - The **MSR projection for FY Y** is in the **September issue of year Y** (labeled "estimate").
  - The **actual for FY Y** is in the **September issue of year Y+1** (FY ends Sep 30; the following September/December issues print the final actual). The question usually names both issues explicitly — use exactly those vintages.

## Reading the figures
- "Projected federal budget" in these questions means the **total budget deficit (or surplus)**, not receipts or outlays.
- The narrative text often states the deficit directly **in trillions** (e.g. "$1.47 trillion"); tables print **millions of dollars**. If reading from the table, convert: millions ÷ 1,000,000 = trillions.
- Watch for multiple estimate columns (e.g. current FY estimate vs. budget-year estimate) — take the column for the asked fiscal year.

## Arithmetic convention
- When the question says values "rounded to the nearest hundredths place in trillions": **round EACH value to 2 decimals first, THEN take the absolute difference** (e.g. projection 1.47, actual 1.30 → |1.47 − 1.30| = 0.17). Do not subtract unrounded millions and round the result — it can differ by 0.01.
- Deficits are compared as magnitudes; report the absolute difference as a positive number with 2 decimals.
