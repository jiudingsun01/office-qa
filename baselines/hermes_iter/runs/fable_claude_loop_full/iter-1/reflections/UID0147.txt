Skill recorded at `.claude/skills/tb-maturity-schedule/SKILL.md`. Here's what the post-mortem found:

**Root cause of the wrong answer.** I located the right table (the "Maturity Schedule of Interest-Bearing Public Marketable Securities … Outstanding January 31" in each March bulletin) but summed the fixed-maturity column across **all** maturity years (55.4, 48.0, 54.6, 57.5 B → OLS 57.0). I verified against the actual bulletins (via the archive.org mirror, since FRASER 403s curl) that the gold answer instead uses the **first year-section's Total row** — fixed-maturity securities maturing within the schedule's own calendar year: 46,625 / 36,068 / ~44,5xx / 40,537 million. Plugging that declining series into the OLS shortcut (ŷ = −0.5·y1 + 0.5·y3 + y4) reproduces 39.5 almost exactly, so the convention is confirmed, not guessed.

**What the new skill captures (all generalizable):**
- Table location and the bulletin-lag rule ("schedule published Jan 31" lives in the March issue).
- Column semantics: fixed vs callable (classified by first call and final maturity — don't double count), and the footnote that bonds with definite notice of call are reclassified as fixed.
- The gold convention: within-year Total row, not the cross-year grand total — with the verified anchor values as a tripwire.
- The exact-weight OLS extrapolation shortcut for 4 equally spaced points.
- The FRASER-blocked → `archive.org/download/sim_treasury-bulletin_YYYY-MM/..._djvu.txt` retrieval tactic, plus the cross-check-adjacent-bulletin trick for garbled OCR cells.

Sources: [Treasury Bulletin, March 1948/1949/1950/1951 OCR via archive.org](https://archive.org/details/sim_treasury-bulletin_1948-03), located through [FRASER's Treasury Bulletin index](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/treasury-bulletin-407).
